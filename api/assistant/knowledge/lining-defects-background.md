# Background: defects in concrete tunnel linings
Source: general engineering background written for this project, not quoted from any standard. Check the relevant code (for example the IS codes or ICOLD guidance) before relying on it.

## Cracks
Longitudinal cracks run along the tunnel; circumferential cracks run around it. Branching or map cracks spread in a network.
Common causes include shrinkage as concrete dries, temperature changes, ground pressure and water pressure inside the tunnel.
Wider cracks let more water through and matter more than hairline cracks. Whether a crack is still growing matters as much as its size.

## Spalling and exposed reinforcement
Spalling is loss of concrete from the surface. It can come from corrosion of steel bars swelling inside the concrete, from impact, or from erosion by fast water carrying sand.
Once reinforcement is exposed, it can corrode faster.

## Leaching and seepage
White deposits (leaching or efflorescence) show that water has been moving through the concrete and carrying dissolved minerals with it.
Seepage is water coming through cracks or joints. This system does not detect seepage.

## Joints are not cracks
Construction joints and ring joints are planned lines in the lining. They look like straight cracks, so the system lets engineers reject them as "ring joint or seam".
